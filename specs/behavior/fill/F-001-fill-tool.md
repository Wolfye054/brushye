## Setup (exact)

### Tolerance
- Clicking the gray ramp at v = 128 (x = 401) with tolerance t fills every column with
  **|v − 128| ≤ T, T = round(2.55·t)** (half up): T = 0, 3, 5, 13, 26, 51, 77 at t = 0, 1, 2,
  5, 10, 20, 30 (`gray_t*`); t ≥ 50 fills the whole ramp. Every row is filled the same.
- The red-only, red/green and alpha ramps give **the same T** at t = 5, 10, 20 (`red_t*`,
  `redgreen_t*`, `alpha_t*`): the distance is the **largest difference over R, G, B and A**
  (Chebyshev), not a sum or a Euclidean distance; alpha counts like a colour channel.
- Not separated yet: whether colours are compared straight or premultiplied (all ramps so far
  give the same answer either way).

### Anti-aliasing
With d the distance (as above) of a pixel to the clicked colour:
- **Off:** binary: exactly the connected pixels with d ≤ T (`ringnoaa_t10`, `ringnoaa_t60`).
- **On, t < 50:** pixels with d ≤ T get 255; the pixels next to the filled area get
  **⌈255·(255 − d)/(255 − T)⌉** (ring, white seed: exact on every anti-aliased pixel at t = 0,
  10, 20, 30, 40, 45, 49, 49.5, 49.8; `ring_t*`). On the gray ramp this is the single column
  beyond each end at alpha 254.
- **On, t ≥ 50:** pixels with d > T get 0; those with d ≤ T get **min(255, ⌊255·(255 − d)/T⌋)**
  (ring, exact at t = 50, 51, 60, 75). On the gray ramp (seed 128) t = 50 fills everything at
  255, which this rule doesn't give (it would give 253 at v = 0): the "255" is probably tied to
  the seed colour. **Open.**

## Run 4 (2026-11-15, pen display :78): To darkest pixel
Data in `data/run4/`; inputs `dk1`–`dk6` (`tools/observe/make_fill_inputs.py`). Same setup as
run 3 (Refer other layers, tolerance 10, Close gap off, click at canvas (20, 20) in the
outside), Scaling mode To darkest pixel, every Area scaling value checked on a screenshot.
- **Inputs.** `dk1`, `dk2`: square outlines (150 px), each with its own profile across the
  thickness, outside → inside: `dk1` [100 50 80 0], [100 50 50 80 0], [0 0 128], [128 0],
  [100 100 100 100], [0 0 0 50 0 0 0], [0 128 0], [100 100 0]; `dk2` [0 30 30 30],
  [0 50 100 150 200], [220 200 … 20 0] (12 px), [50 0 50 0 50], [0 1], [1 0], [100 0 0 100],
  [100 100 100] with a black interior; all with white interiors otherwise. `dk3` (2-D): 1 px
  and 2 px diagonal black lines, a black ring 3 px thick, a diagonal valley band, a radial
  valley ring, a black plus sign. `dk4`: two-pixel colour pairs [A, B] (red/blue, blue/red,
  green/red, gray 80/red, gray 30/blue, gray 170/green, gray 100/blue, gray 110/magenta).
  `dk5`, `dk6`: black at alpha 128/200/255, white and grays on a transparent background
  (RGBA 0 0 0 0) with transparent interiors.
- **Captures.** Anti-aliasing off: `dk1`–`dk3` at N = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, `dk4`–`dk6` at 10,
  and each unscaled (`<img>_d<N>_no`, `<img>_base_no`). Anti-aliasing on: `dk1` at 1–5, 10–15,
  20, `dk2`/`dk3` at 3, 10, 20 (`_aa`).
- **The Area scaling field stops at 20** (30, 50 and 100 typed in To darkest pixel mode each
  show 20). The lower bound and the other modes' bounds were not checked.
- **Rule (anti-aliasing off), exact on all 21 scaled captures and on run 3's 10 To darkest pixel
  captures (`sc_darkest*`, `ol_darkest*`), 0 pixels different:** starting from the unscaled
  fill, N steps; each step adds every pixel q outside the area that has a 4-neighbour p in
  the area (as it was before the step) such that **q is darker than p**, or **q has the same
  tone as p and the pixel beyond q (q + (q − p)) is not lighter than q**. Growth stops
  early when a step adds nothing.
  - So solid shapes grow by the diamond (N 4-neighbour steps: the run 3 disk), lines are
    entered down to their darkest pixel, and a flat run followed by something lighter loses
    its last pixel (uniform line t → t − 1; t = 1 is entered since its pixel is darker
    than the white it's reached from). [100 50 80 0] stops after 50; [100 100 0] is entered
    entirely; [0 128 0] stops after the first 0.
- **Darkness of a pixel:** compared on **BT.601 luma** (0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B) of the
  straight colour **and alpha separately**. q is "not lighter" than p when its luma is no
  higher and its alpha no lower; "darker" when it is not lighter and not the same.
  - `dk4`: blue after red, red after green, red after gray 80, blue after gray 30, green
    after gray 170, magenta after gray 110 are all entered (darker); red after blue is not.
    This excludes the RGB mean, the max and BT.709 weights (green 182 > 170).
  - `dk5`: transparent → black, 50 % black → black, black at alpha 200 → black are entered;
    black → 50 % black is not; transparent → white → black enters both (a fully transparent
    pixel counts as **white**: lighter than everything, as light as white).
  - `dk6`: 50 % black and opaque gray 126, 127, 129, 130 (and gray 128 in `dk5`) are each
    **not** entered from the other, in both directions: luma and alpha are not traded off
    (composited over white, 50 % black would be 127).
- **Negative N:** run 3's `ol_darkest-1`, `ol_darkest-3` equal the shrink by the square
  (8-neighbour erosion), not the diamond (32 and 192 pixels off); on these outlines the
  Rectangle and Round shrinks are identical.
- **Anti-aliasing on:** thresholding the anti-aliased result at 128 gives the
  anti-aliasing-off area (0 pixels different, except 16 on `dk1`/`ol` at N ≥ 10). The soft
  pixels are the grown outline smoothed: on a side where the last pixel of a flat run was left
  out, that pixel ramps linearly along the side from 127 at the corners to 1 (N ≤ 11) or 2
  (N ≥ 12) at the middle; the diamond's corners become 45° anti-aliased steps (239 207 175
  143 at N = 10). **Open:** the smoothing rule (the same as run 3's curve smoothing?).

## Run 5 (2026-11-15, pen display :78): anti-aliased smoothing of scaled areas
Data in `data/run5/`; input `sm1` (`tools/observe/make_fill_inputs.py`): black shapes on white,
the fill clicked in the white at canvas (20, 20) and grown into them; Refer other layers,
tolerance 10, Close gap off. Shapes: right triangles whose hypotenuse is a staircase of slope
1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/8, 2/3, 3/4, 2/5, 0.37; 140×60 rectangles with, on the top side, one
step of 1, 2, 3 px, a 1 px notch 1, 3, 6 px wide, a 2 px bump 3, 6 px wide; squares 1–5 px;
disks r 3, 5, 8, 12. Captures, anti-aliasing on (`_aa`) and off (`_no`): Rectangle 1, 3, −1,
−3, 10; Round 1, 3; To darkest pixel 1, 3, −1, −3, 10; plus unscaled (`sm1_<mode><N>_<aa>`).
Analysis: `tools/observe/analyze_fill_smoothing.py`.
- **The smoothing depends only on the binary area.** Where two captures have the same
  anti-aliasing-off area (Rectangle 1 / Round 1, Rectangle 3 / Round 3, Rectangle −1 /
  To darkest pixel −1, Rectangle −3 / To darkest pixel −3), their anti-aliased results are
  identical (0 pixels). Anti-aliasing on, thresholded at 128, gives the anti-aliasing-off
  area (≤ 5 pixels differ per capture). It applies to growing and to shrinking.
- **Staircases (exact):** the anti-aliased edge is the exact area coverage, round(255·c), of
  the straight line through the **midpoints of the outline's straight runs** (horizontal and
  vertical runs of pixel edges). On the middle of the ten hypotenuses, in Rectangle 1, 3 and To
  darkest pixel 1, 3, 2750 of 2802 soft pixels match: every slope, irregular ones (2/3, 3/4,
  2/5, 0.37) included. Exceptions: slope 1/5 gives 152 where the exact value is 153.0 (CSP's
  coverage arithmetic is a hair below exact; elsewhere round-half-up matches, 31.875 → 32,
  223.125 → 223); slope 1/8 at N = 3 differs next to the triangle's corner (below). Values
  seen: 45° 223/32 (7/8, 1/8), 1/2 191/64, 1/3 244 170 85 11.
- **Long runs keep their corners.** Rectangle corners and the triangles' corners between
  long legs stay sharp (no soft pixels), so the midpoint rule alone (which would cut every
  corner) does not hold where long runs meet.
- **Single features on a long edge become long straight ramps** ending at the midpoints of
  the neighbouring long runs (Rectangle 1, rows as observed):
  - a 1 px step between runs of 68 and 70 px: the edge stays straight from the rectangle's
    corner (21, 381) to the left run's midpoint (55, 381), then one straight line through the
    right run's midpoint (124, 382) continues to the rectangle's right side, meeting it at
    (159, 382.5) (the soft values fall 125 → 2 across the right run);
  - steps of 2 and 3 px: the same ramps on both sides, plus 45°-like transitions at the step
    (64, 120/133; 85, 11/244, 101/133);
  - a 3 px notch (1 px deep): ramps from both runs' midpoints rising to 127 at the notch,
    159 159 inside it; notches 5 and 8 px wide: 143 207 207 143 and 135 167 199 231 231 199
    167 135 inside;
  - bumps: the same, mirrored (the bump pixels 64 / 191, 120; ramps 125 → 2 / 4).
- **Hypotheses tried, not matching:** (a) the polygon through every run's midpoint: exact on
  staircases, wrong at long-run corners (a square would become a diamond); (b) an optimal
  polygon with vertex adjustment, following the published Potrace paper (Selinger 2003): with
  either a half-pixel or a cone straightness test it takes the stepped top side as **one**
  segment (CSP uses two, bending at the left run's midpoint), and it misses the notches and
  bumps (136–204 pixels off per cell). The rule at long runs is **open**.

## Run 6 (2026-11-16, pen display :78): smoothing where long runs meet
Data in `data/run6/`; input `sm2` (`tools/observe/make_fill_inputs.py`): 27 cells of 190×100,
each a black rectangle (150×50 unless stated) with one feature on its top side, clicked at
canvas (5, 5): a 1 px step down at column 10, 20, 40, 75, 110, 130; the mirrored step (up) at
20, 75, 130; a centred step on rectangles 20, 40, 80, 170 wide; two 1 px steps 5, 10, 30,
60 apart; 1 px deep notches and 1 px high bumps 10, 20, 40 wide; a step on the left, right and
bottom side of a 90×80 rectangle; a plain rectangle. Captures: Rectangle 1 and 3,
anti-aliasing on and off, and unscaled. Anti-aliasing on thresholded at 128 equals off
(0 pixels). Analysis: `tools/observe/analyze_fill_longruns.py`; the boundary is read per column
as the sum of the fill coverage (the mean height of the smoothed edge over the column), and
lines are fitted to it.
- **Observed, a single step** (runs at heights y (upper) and y + 1 (lower), the step at
  column s, all exact to the fit's precision for every position and width):
  - **Lower run** (the side where the edge is one row further into the shape): one straight
    line through the step's midpoint (s, y + ½) and the lower run's midpoint, continued to
    the run's far corner, which it meets **½ px past the hard edge** (y + 1½). E.g. step at
    10 on a 148 px edge: through (8, 26.5) and (78, 27) to (148, 27.5).
  - **Upper run**: flat from its far corner to its midpoint, then a straight ramp to the
    step's midpoint, the ramp **translated ¼ px along the run, away from the step**: it
    leaves the edge at midpoint − ¼ and reaches y + ½ at s − ¼ (3.75 / 7.75, 8.75 / 17.75,
    18.75 / 37.75, 53.75 / 107.75 for steps at 8, 18, 38, 108).
  - Mirrored cells mirror exactly; steps on the left, right and bottom sides behave the same
    (orientation does not matter).
  - Which run is "lower" decides it: the continuation to the corner happens where it moves
    the boundary into the shape (the fill grows); toward the other corner the edge stays
    flat. A corner between two runs whose other ends are corners stays sharp.
- **Two steps:** the middle run goes straight between the two step midpoints (as in a
  staircase); the outer runs follow the single-step rule.
- **Notches and bumps:** the inner run makes a V: two lines from the step midpoints toward
  the inner run's midpoint, each translated ¼ px into the inner run (notch 10: through
  (68.25, 26.5) and (79.75, 26.5), apex 26.979 instead of 27); the outer runs follow the
  upper-run rule.
- **Model:** run midpoints; corners between runs of length ≥ 2 kept, except the lower-run
  continuation; the ¼ px translation of step-to-run segments that are not continued to a
  corner (not in monotone staircases). On `sm2` it reproduces 2378 of 2649 soft pixels exactly
  at N = 1 and 2317 of 2600 at N = 3, the rest within ±3 (most ±1; ±1 differences have no
  pattern in the exact value, so CSP's coverage arithmetic or vertex precision differs
  slightly). On run 5's staircases it stays exact.
- **Not covered by the model (open):**
  - **acute tips** where a staircase turns back into a long run (the triangles' corners in
    `sm1`): CSP keeps the long run flat up to the tip and continues the staircase's line;
  - **2 and 3 px steps** (run 5 `step2`, `step3`): CSP treats them like 1 px steps (45° cut
    64 at the step, ramps on both sides), while the model's corner rule takes them as long
    runs; raising the "long" length for corners to 3 or 4 breaks staircases and tips, so the
    two notions differ;
  - small shapes (run 5's 1–5 px squares, r 3–12 disks) not yet compared.

## Implementation notes (Brushye)
- Anti-aliasing border: CSP softens only the **4-neighbours** of the region (diagonal
  neighbours stay 0: 184 ring pixels at t = 0), and at t ≥ 50 only region pixels with a
  4-neighbour outside it (the gray ramp has none, so it stays 255). With these two, every
  export in runs 1–2 is reproduced exactly.
- Area scaling Rectangle / Round (`scale=`, `scale_mode=`): the binary region grown or shrunk
  with the selection morphology (square, rounded kernel), exact without anti-aliasing and on
  straight edges with it; the smoothing of curved edges is not modelled.
- To darkest pixel (`scale_mode=darkest`, the default mode): run 4's rule, exact without
  anti-aliasing. Negative N shrinks by the square. Refer other layers / Refer to all layers
  default to To darkest pixel 10, as in CSP. Chosen, not observed: a pixel beyond the canvas
  edge counts as darkest (a flat run reaching the edge is entered to the edge); luma is
  compared unrounded (×1000), so colours whose 8-bit luma would tie are still ordered.
- Smoothing (anti-aliasing on, any scaled area, `brushye_core::smooth`): the run 5–6 model
  (run midpoints; kept corners between runs ≥ 2 long; the lower-run continuation to the
  corner; the ¼ px translation of step segments) plus one rule read from the existing
  captures: **both corners of a short run that turns the outline around** (its neighbours
  run in opposite directions) are kept, so the long runs beside it stay straight. That is
  the end of a 1 px black line left inside run 3's 3 px bar (CSP: no soft pixels; without the
  rule the whole line was slanted) and the triangles' tips in run 5. With it, run 3's bars
  are exact and run 5's To darkest pixel errors fall from 946 to 93 pixels.
  - Agreement, per capture (`area_scaling_smoothing` in `observed_fill.rs`, soft pixels
    exact / pixels off by more than 1 / largest error): `sm2` Rectangle 1 2378/105/3,
    Rectangle 3 2317/108/3; run 3 disk (Rectangle 5, Round 10, To darkest pixel 10) at most
    16 pixels off by 2; run 4 `dk3` 912/0/1, `outlines` 2744/16/4; `dk1` 2244/24/112 (the
    diamond's 45° corner smoothing is drawn at two left corners where CSP leaves them sharp);
    `sm1` 895–2277 exact, 84–466 off, up to 154: its 2–3 px steps (run 5 `step2`, `step3`,
    the 2 px bumps). Treating 2–3 px steps between long runs as 1 px steps was tried and
    made them worse (errors up to 255); it is not the rule.
  - Chosen, not observed: beyond the canvas edge the area repeats its edge pixels, so the
    canvas border is never smoothed. Coverage is exact area, rounded half up.
  - Cost: about 0.3 s for a 4000×4000 area (release build).
- Chosen, not observed: 4-connectivity of the region; Close gap as a box closing with boxes
  2, 4, 6, 11, 21 px (reproduces every hold/leak outcome; the fill's shape inside a closed
  gap is approximate); a fill inside a selection is clipped like a stroke.
